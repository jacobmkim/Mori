## 1. Personal post (Jacob — founder voice) → post Tuesday morning

I've sold exactly zero subscriptions, and I've already spent two weeks writing the code that handles the money.

That sounds backwards. It isn't.

When someone eventually pays for Mori's upcoming tier, whether they're actually premium can't be decided by the app on their phone — a phone can be fooled, and a dropped network call at the wrong moment either locks out a paying customer or hands the paid tier to someone who didn't pay. So the real source of truth lives on a server, and every purchase, renewal, cancel, refund and billing retry has to flow through one webhook that's the *only* thing allowed to flip the "premium" switch.

I ran an adversarial review over that one file before merging it. It found roughly eight bugs — each one a quiet way to lose money or wrongly cut someone off.

None of this shows up in a demo. Nobody screenshots a webhook. But it's the difference between a business and a leak.

The unglamorous truth of solo-founding: the code that touches money is the code you least get to rush.

## 2. Mori company-page post → post Thursday morning

Most recipe apps brag about "millions of recipes." We deliberately stopped at 2,600+.

Here's why that's a feature, not a shortfall.

An open firehose of scraped recipes means half of them have vague steps, missing amounts, or macros nobody checked. When your meal planner is only as good as the meals inside it, "more" quietly becomes "worse." So every recipe in Mori is curated: real step-by-step instructions with timing cues, ingredient amounts written the way you'd actually shop for them, and estimated macros grounded in USDA data — always labeled estimated, because we won't pretend a number is clinical when it isn't.

That curation is what makes the rest of the app honest. When Mori learns your taste from swipes and plans your week, it's choosing from a shelf where every option is one we'd actually cook — not a lottery of internet recipes.

Quality you can taste beats quantity you have to sift through.

2,600+ tested recipes, free on the US and Canada App Store.
