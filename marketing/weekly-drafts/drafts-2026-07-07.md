## 1. Personal post (Jacob — founder voice) → post Tuesday morning

I can't afford a QA team. So I built one that attacks me.

Every feature I ship now goes through the same gauntlet: I spin up dozens of AI agents whose only job is to break what I just built — one hunts security holes, one hunts race conditions, one plays the angriest possible user. Then a second round of agents tries to *refute* the first round's findings, because agents flag ghosts constantly and I only fix what survives cross-examination.

This week's haul, on one feature: 9 confirmed real bugs before a single user touched it. Including the humbling one — my rate limiter had a read-then-write race, meaning enough simultaneous requests could all slip through. It had "worked" for months. It had never actually worked.

Here's the part I keep chewing on: the agents aren't smarter than me. They're just tireless and adversarial in a way I can't be about my own code at 11pm. Solo founders have always been told the weakness is "no second pair of eyes." That excuse is expiring.

The bottleneck now isn't finding bugs. It's having the discipline to run the gauntlet when you'd rather just ship.

## 2. Mori company-page post → post Thursday morning

The fastest way to ruin a grocery delivery: make someone buy salt they already own.

A recipe app that dumps every ingredient into your cart isn't saving you time — it's charging you for your own pantry. So Mori sweats the last ten feet of the handoff:

Shelf-stable staples — dry spices, vinegars, oils, sweeteners — get skipped automatically, with a note showing exactly what was left out. Items in your pantry ship by default, but each one carries a little tap-to-skip pill, so *you* make the call (we learned the hard way that silently dropping eggs from an egg soufflé is not a favor). And "4 salmon fillets" gets sent as 1.5 lb, so the store fills the right amount instead of guessing at package counts.

None of it is glamorous. All of it is the difference between a cart you trust and a cart you have to audit.

That's the promise end to end: swipe on food you actually want, Mori learns your taste, plans your week — and the groceries that show up are the ones you actually needed.

Free on the US and Canada App Store.
