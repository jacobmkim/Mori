## 1. Personal post (Jacob — founder voice) → post Tuesday morning

I spent this week teaching my app to learn from the meals people *reject*, not just the ones they like.

The obvious way to build a meal planner is to weight what people swipe right on. But I kept watching my own behavior: I'd accept an auto-planned week, then quietly swap out Tuesday because I'd had pasta on Sunday and didn't want it again. The swipe said "yes, I like pasta." The swap said something the swipe never could — "not this week, not in this slot."

So the bigger signal isn't the like. It's the correction. When you swap a planned dinner for the next-best option, that's you teaching the planner in real time, and ignoring it would be the whole bug.

It's slower to build. A "learn from what you change" loop is more state, more edge cases, more ways to be wrong than a static recommender. But a planner that doesn't notice you keep overriding it isn't smart — it's just confident.

Still pre-launch. Still dogfooding my own dinners. But this is the part I'd have skipped if I were rushing.

## 2. Mori company-page post → post Thursday morning

Every macro number in Mori started as a question: per serving of what, exactly?

It's easy to slap a calorie count on a recipe. It's harder to make it mean something. So behind the 2,600+ recipes in Mori, the estimated macros are grounded against USDA reference data — portion by portion, ingredient by ingredient — and re-checked when something looks off. A "4 chicken thighs" gets read as the weight it actually is, not a guess. Cheese doesn't get double-counted. The number you see is one we've actually tried to stand behind (and yes — always labelled estimated, because real kitchens vary).

Why bother? Because the whole promise only works if you trust it. You swipe on dinners you actually want, Mori learns your taste, plans your week, and ships the list to Instacart. If the macros under that are vibes, the plan is vibes too.

No staring into the fridge at 6pm. No math at the counter. Just food you'll cook, with numbers you can lean on.

Free on the US and Canada App Store.
