## 1. Personal post (Jacob — founder voice) → post Tuesday morning

I wrote a launch rule this month that can kill my own launch.

If I don't cook at least 40% of the dinners my app plans for me over one week, the feature doesn't ship. No exceptions, no "we'll patch it after launch."

I'm building an auto-planning tier for Mori — it learns your taste from swipes and drafts your whole week for you. On paper it demos beautifully. But a meal plan isn't a screenshot. Its only honest success metric is whether food actually got cooked. A gorgeous plan you ignore is worse than no plan — it's a weekly reminder that the app doesn't get you.

So I'm the first guinea pig. One week, my real kitchen, my real Tuesday-night tiredness. If the number comes in under 40%, the right move is to ship a smaller, truer version and keep working — not to dress a demo up as a product.

Vanity metrics are easy to hit. Cooked dinners are not.

That's the whole reason I set the gate before I knew if I'd pass it.

## 2. Mori company-page post → post Thursday morning

Most recipe apps hand you a 20-question quiz before you've seen a single meal.

Mori just watches you swipe.

Left on the mushroom curry, right on the miso salmon, right again on the sheet-pan chicken — and quietly, a picture of your taste starts forming. No "rate your interest in Mediterranean cuisine from 1 to 5." Just the honest signal of what you'd actually eat tonight.

Under the hood, every swipe updates a taste score that runs right on your phone — weighing the cuisines you lean toward, the ingredients you keep passing on, your dietary needs, even what you cooked last week so dinner doesn't repeat itself. The more you swipe, the sharper the deck gets.

It's the first half of the whole Mori idea: you never decide what's for dinner again — it learns your taste, plans your week, and ships the groceries to Instacart.

2,600+ recipes, each with full step-by-steps and estimated macros. Free on the US and Canada App Store.
