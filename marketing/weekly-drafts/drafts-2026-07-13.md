## 1. Personal post (Jacob — founder voice) → post Tuesday morning

My meal planner had perfect taste and zero memory. It proposed the same seven dinners. Every. Single. Week.

I'm building auto-planning for Mori's upcoming paid tier — it learns your taste from swipes and drafts your week. The taste scoring worked. Too well. The engine picked the mathematically "best" recipe for every slot, and since your taste doesn't change much week to week, neither did the plan. Week 3 looked exactly like week 1.

The embarrassing part: every past plan was already saved in the database. The planner just never read them back. It had a perfect diary and total amnesia.

The fix was three small layers: penalize anything you were served in the last 3 weeks (graded — last week hurts more than three weeks ago), protect 1–2 proven favourites as anchors so it doesn't rotate away the meal you actually love, and sample from the top 3 candidates instead of always taking #1.

The lesson I keep relearning: personalization without memory isn't personalization. It's a rut with good intentions.

This is exactly why I dogfood-gate the launch — if I don't cook what it plans, nobody else will either.

## 2. Mori company-page post → post Thursday morning

Mori knows how long 1,769 different ingredients last — in the fridge, the freezer, or on the counter.

Because the recipe is only half the job. The other half is the half-block of tofu and the open can of coconut milk staring at you two days later.

So when you finish cooking in Mori, we ask one small question: "What's left?" Tag your leftovers and Mori quietly does the rest —

It knows the shelf life of each ingredient and gives you the right storage call (that fresh ginger? freezer, not fridge). It nudges you *before* something turns, not after. And here's our favorite part: your discovery deck starts gently surfacing recipes that use up what's about to expire — so Tuesday's spinach becomes Thursday's dinner instead of Friday's compost.

No spreadsheets, no barcode scanning, no guilt. Just a plan that remembers what's in your kitchen.

That's the whole idea behind Mori: you never decide what's for dinner again — it learns your taste, plans your week, and ships the groceries.

Free on the US and Canada App Store.
